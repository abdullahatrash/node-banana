# Build a focused video editor inside Tasmeemai

Tasmeemai will build its own focused Video Editor inside the existing application instead of delegating `/editor` to OpenCut through a separately deployed microfrontend. The concrete use case is combining a generated AI influencer reaction with the creator's phone screen recording or camera footage. Ease of use, performance, and operating cost take priority over expanding into a full production suite, accepting ownership of a smaller editing implementation instead of adopting OpenCut's broader editor.

This supersedes the architectural direction in the March 27 OpenCut integration design and implementation plan. Existing OpenCut routing remains in the code until replacement is implemented; this decision does not claim that migration or performance validation is complete.

The app's video output is exclusively 9:16 short form; 16:9 landscape output is outside the product direction. The editor combines two videos using preset layouts: a reaction overlay, side-by-side panels, and stacked top-and-bottom panels. The creator selects when the reaction starts; it plays once and disappears when it finishes while the main footage continues. Reactions usually last 5–10 seconds and at most 15 seconds; this does not limit the main footage to 15 seconds.

The initial editing experience targets ordinary laptops and 1080p export. Equally capable phone editing is outside the initial target. Performance is a release requirement, covering opening speed, editing responsiveness, preview playback, memory use, and an interface that remains usable during export. Initial prototype measurements exist on a capable development laptop; ordinary-laptop qualification and operating-cost measurements remain incomplete.

The narrowed workflow has a maximum finished duration of 60 seconds, retaining the 15-second reaction limit. Simple text overlays are included initially; automatic subtitles and transcription are deferred. A throwaway browser-export performance prototype is authorized before choosing production export infrastructure. It must exercise two video inputs, Arabic text, Music, and Voiceover and measure export speed, memory, and interface responsiveness; browser export requires keeping the tab open. Passing on one development computer is not qualification on an ordinary laptop.

Text overlays must be editable directly on the video preview and movable within the video frame. Basic formatting includes multiple lines, left/center/right alignment, thin/light/regular/bold weights, font size, text color, and background color with opacity. Formatting applies to the whole overlay. The exported video must retain the edited text, styling, and chosen placement.

The throwaway experiment is captured on `feature/throwaway-reaction-export` at `src/app/editor/throwaway-export/RESULTS.md`. On an M1 Max with 32 GiB RAM, headed Chrome initially exported each 60-second layout in approximately 5.1–5.2 seconds with responsive sampled UI interactions. A later stacked export with local conversion of 44.1 kHz WAV music and 24 kHz WAV voiceover took 5.4 seconds, including preparation and the updated text controls. This establishes feasibility on that machine only. Approximately 75 ms of AAC/container duration excess remains unresolved; a separate five-second marker diagnostic finds 44 ms of leading audio delay even without the video mixer. Some MP3 files are rejected by the installed library's format detector. Production export execution remains undecided; the experiment does not authorize shipping the prototype or purchasing render infrastructure. The user subsequently accepted the measured 44 ms audio delay for now and deferred its correction. It is a known limitation rather than a current release blocker; this acceptance does not establish identical timing across browsers or resolve MP3 compatibility.

The editor includes at least two independently controlled added audio layers: Music and Voiceover. These are distinct from any audio embedded in the main footage or influencer reaction; source-audio retention and mixing rules still need to be settled. Music and Voiceover have separate sections, each accepting an uploaded audio file or existing Workspace audio initially. Direct microphone recording is deferred. Both sections must accommodate AI-generated audio later.

AI music and voiceover generation is explicitly deferred. Suno and ElevenLabs were named as examples of possible future providers, not selected integrations. Reserve a place in the interaction design for generation within the corresponding audio section; keep the editing model based on audio media references rather than provider-specific controls. Initial delivery must not require provider connections or generation calls. Precise timing, volume, looping, and ducking behavior remain open.

Workspace autosave and initial input support up to 1080p were agreed earlier. Final export execution was initially agreed as server-side background work, then reopened when operating-cost concerns prompted a first-principles review of the use case; browser versus server export remains under reconsideration pending the prototype. The earlier ten-clip, three-minute general-editor scope is superseded by this two-video, 60-second workflow. Music and Voiceover layers have been explicitly reconfirmed for the narrower workflow.

Audio behavior, crop and framing controls, panel behavior after the reaction ends, main-footage limits, retained optional features, reference laptop and browser, numeric performance budgets, export execution, storage accounting, and migration details remain open for the design interview. The application-wide 9:16 decision requires a later audit of existing video formats; this documentation change does not claim their implementation has been updated. Other product parity commitments are unaffected.
